Where appointments leak

  1. The phone rings during a fade. Nobody picks up, and the caller books the next shop on Google Maps.

  2. The site says “call to book”. A first-time client at 10 PM won't call. They'll keep scrolling.

  3. A popup covers the menu. Ten percent off, enter your email. Plenty of people close the tab before they see a price.

  4. A demand letter shows up. Thousands of ADA website lawsuits are filed every year, and small shops get them too.

Questions owners ask us first.

I already use Square, Vagaro or Booksy.

Keep it. We put your booking link behind every Book button and style the site around it. If you'd rather own the booking and stop paying per seat, we can move you to ours.

Will this make my site ADA compliant?

We build and test every page to WCAG 2.1 AA, the benchmark courts and regulators use, and publish an Accessibility Statement with a way to reach you. Nobody can honestly promise you'll never get a demand letter. A tested site gives it much less to stand on.

Who writes the text?

We do, from facts you give us: how long you've been open, who works there, what people ask on the phone. You'll get about ten questions from us, and you approve every word before launch.

Can clients get text reminders?

Yes, if they check the box when they book. Opt-in only, which keeps you clear of TCPA trouble. Replies land in your phone.

What does it cost after launch?

From $49 a month for hosting, backups and small edits. If you'd rather host it yourself, we hand over the files.

Do you use stock photos?

Your own photos come first, even phone shots: we edit them to one look. Stock fills gaps only, and never pretends to be your team or your work.
